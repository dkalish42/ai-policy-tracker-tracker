Coverage Gap Analysis

What the trackers collectively miss — or at least don't cover well.

Municipal & City AI Ordinances Moderate Gap

Cities like NYC, San Jose, and Baltimore have passed local AI rules. The NLC City AI Governance Dashboard is a start, but coverage remains thin. Most trackers still stop at the state line.

Enforcement Actions & Outcomes Moderate Gap

Plenty of trackers list what passed. Akin and Holistic AI track enforcement and litigation, and Troutman's new Sentinel report now covers state-AG AI enforcement. But systematic coverage of penalties assessed and compliance rates remains thin. Still largely a "so what" gap.

AI Procurement Rules Moderate Gap

Government AI procurement standards and requirements are scattered across agency-specific guidance. No single tracker aggregates them comprehensively.

Sector-Specific Trackers Moderate Gap

Healthcare is now well covered (Manatt, Ropes & Gray, Holland & Knight), insurance has the NAIC tracker, and Epstein Becker Green's new map adds an employment slice. ICMA's log follows capital-markets regulators outside the US, but US financial services and defense/intel AI rules remain fragmented.

Soft Law & Voluntary Commitments Moderate Gap

White House AI commitments, industry pledges, standards body frameworks — the OECD tracks some, and AlgorithmWatch cataloged 160+ ethical guidelines before going dormant in 2024. The full universe of non-binding AI governance remains poorly mapped.

Global South AI Governance Moderate Gap

Africa, Latin America, and Southeast Asia are actively legislating AI, and dedicated regional trackers now exist for each (the Africa AI Policy Tool, ILIA and the Uniandes/IDB regulation repository, and the SEA AI Observatory), alongside the revived Global Index on Responsible AI. But most stay high-level: deep sectoral analysis of non-Western jurisdictions remains thin, and the Gulf/MENA region still has no dedicated tracker at all.

Litigation & Case Law Moderate Gap

AI-related lawsuits are shaping policy as fast as legislation. Dedicated case trackers have multiplied (DAIL, the copyright trackers, Hogan Lovells Cadwalader, Mishcon de Reya, the TRAL tech litigation tracker, Damien Charlotin's database of AI-hallucination rulings, the independent AI Lawsuit Tracker, and Fisher Phillips' workplace litigation dashboard), but systematic tracking of outcomes and remedies across them remains sparse.

Real-Time Regulatory Guidance Minor Gap

Agency guidance, interpretive letters, and FAQ updates move fast and often matter more than statutes. A few trackers cover this, but most focus on enacted law.
